Decision gates

A decision gate pauses consequential work at a named boundary. It evaluates a concrete proposal; it is not a general “trust this agent” switch.

What a useful proposal contains​

A reviewer or policy needs enough stable context to decide:

  • tenant, principal, run, and immutable revision;
  • exact operation and target;
  • arguments or a safe summary of them;
  • declared risk, effects, and idempotency;
  • selected connection or placement where relevant;
  • cost and time bounds;
  • expiry and the consequence of no decision.

Changing a decision-relevant field produces a new proposal. An approval of one comment body should not authorize a different body because both use the same operation.

Decision outcomes​

OutcomeLifecycle consequence
Approveresume the exact suspended proposal while it remains valid
Denyterminate or return a typed refusal to the requesting policy
Modifycreate a new proposal or explicitly constrained request; never mutate approval history
Expirerefuse resumption and require fresh evaluation
Cancelclose the pending work under the run's cancellation rules

Human and automated decisions​

The same boundary can support customer-configured automated policy, a named human reviewer, or a combination. The evidence records which mechanism decided, which policy version applied, and which proposal hash or identity was evaluated.

Automation is not automatically weaker, and human review is not automatically sufficient. Both need narrow input, authenticated authority, bounded validity, and an attributable outcome.

Stale decisions stay stale​

Approval should not survive a changed target, revised arguments, expired connection, widened effect set, or replaced agent revision unless policy explicitly creates a new decision. Resumption checks that the suspended request is still the request that was approved.

Gate placement​

Products decide which consequences require a product gate. Connectors still evaluates its grant at invocation, and Substrate still evaluates execution admission when required. Putting a human in the loop does not collapse those independent controls.
