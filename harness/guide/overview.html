A task-fit harness for real engineering work

Begin with the outcome, not a preconfigured bag of tools. The harness shapes a workspace, worker, capabilities, decision boundary, and evidence plan around the task you actually need to solve.

Product pre-v1Work controllable dev coreDefault bounded

The idea​

Most coding-agent experiences begin with a model and whatever tools happen to be installed. Daemonloom begins with the engineering task.

The coding harness is the local-first control plane around that loop. It gives a capable coding worker enough room to investigate, edit, test, and evaluate inside an admitted boundary. When the next useful action would cross that boundary—by widening access, spending more budget, pushing, deploying, or mutating an external system—the harness stops for a deliberate decision.

The goal is not maximum prompting. It is maximum useful autonomy inside a boundary you can see.

Two ways to begin​

The product direction supports an autonomous posture, where the harness observes only admitted state and proposes useful goals, and a prompt posture, where the engineer normally starts with a request. In both, the person can select, edit, defer, reject, steer, or cancel. Initiative changes who suggests the next task; it never widens workspace, network, datasource, operation, or effect authority.

Those postures sit inside versioned Work Modes for understanding, changing, operating, responding, or deciding. The high-level experience is defined, while the Work Mode executor and autonomous proposal surface remain under development. See current status for the exact line.

The engineer's experience​

A complete harness session should feel like one continuous piece of engineering work:

  1. Describe the outcome. State the problem, relevant context, constraints, and completion evidence.
  2. Review the proposed working set. See the workspace posture, available sources, admitted operations, worker, network boundary, time and cost limits, and approval rules.
  3. Admit the boundary once. Routine exploration and iteration continue without repeated permission prompts.
  4. Follow and steer live work. Output, operations, decisions, warnings, and usage remain visible while the task is active.
  5. Decide on boundary changes. Approve or deny the exact proposed change rather than granting broad trust to the worker.
  6. Accept an evidence-backed result. Finish with the diff or recommendation, checks, observed external effects, unresolved uncertainty, and a reversible checkpoint where applicable.

The target supervisor is durable and attachable: the engineer can follow, steer, cancel, detach, and later inspect the same attributable session. Current status distinguishes this product direction from the narrower development paths available today.

What gets shaped for each task​

PartProduct question
OutcomeWhat must be understood, changed, restored, or decided?
WorkspaceWhich repository, worktree, files, and generated artifacts may be read or changed?
SourcesWhich CI, issue, log, cluster, database, or documentation contexts are relevant?
OperationsWhich actions are available, and which remain read-only or approval-gated?
WorkerWhich supported coding harness or model path fits this task?
BoundsWhat are the network, time, resource, cost, and cancellation limits?
EvidenceWhat observations are required before the result is credible?

Detection alone never makes a source usable. A repository remote, cluster context, or service may be visible without being connected, authorized, or admitted to this task.

One surface, different engineering work​

  • Software engineering: understand a codebase, implement a bounded change, run checks, review a patch, or prepare delivery.
  • Infrastructure work: correlate configuration, deployments, jobs, logs, and health without handing service credentials to the coding worker.
  • Incident response: maintain facts and hypotheses, perform approved diagnostics, record a timeline, and verify mitigation or recovery.
  • Complex problem solving: investigate cross-system failures, architecture tradeoffs, migrations, security findings, and other work whose next step is not known in advance.

The working loop stays recognizable across those modes. The task-fit boundary and the evidence of completion change.

What it is not​

It is not a privileged shell wrapped around chat, a universal automation account, or a promise that an agent answer is correct. It does not turn detected tooling into authority, treat an approval as proof of success, silently switch workers or credential paths after failure, or equate process exit with engineering completion.

The engineer owns the objective and consequential decisions. The harness owns the controllable work loop. Agent, Connectors, and Substrate preserve lifecycle, external authority, credentials, execution bounds, and evidence without forcing the engineer to operate unrelated control planes.
